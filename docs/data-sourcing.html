A hand-researched sample built to find out which fields in the ChurchFinder schema can actually be filled — and which ones no amount of crawling will reach.

The README has the two hard filters backwards

The plan lists size and age as equally hard, both needing per-church research. In practice they are nothing alike. Founding years came back for nine of ten churches on the first search — heritage plaques, diocesan records, the Canadian Encyclopedia and Wikipedia all carry them, because a church's own history is the thing a church most likes to publish. Current attendance came back for zero of ten. Not one congregation publishes it.

Age / year founded
No public source → Widely documented. Hard to define, not to find.
Size / attendance
Needs estimation → Genuinely absent. Nothing to estimate from.

That reordering matters for the plan: the age filter is close to shippable from public data, and the size filter needs a different design altogether.

What this changes in the schema

Four things the sample surfaced that the current ticket schema can't represent.

Split year_founded into congregation and building

Every historic church in the sample has two dates, and they diverge wildly — St. James is a 1797 congregation in an 1853 building; Grace Toronto is a 1992 congregation in an 1878 building. "Is this a century-old parish or a five-year-old plant?" is a question about the congregation, so that's what the age filter must use. Storing one ambiguous year would silently mix the two and make the filter lie. And don't assume ordering: St. George's building is twelve years older than its congregation.

Don't ship attendance_estimate as a number

Zero of ten churches publish current attendance, and the numbers that do exist are seating capacity or decades stale. A "500–2000" bucket built on that would be fabrication with a filter UI on top. Two honest options: drop the size filter from v1, or redefine it as seating capacity, which heritage records genuinely carry — and label it that way in the UI. Capacity is a real, sourceable, different thing.

denomination needs a normalization table, not a text column

The sample already breaks flat matching: Metropolitan United was founded Methodist Episcopal and became United at the 1925 church union; Grace Toronto is PCA, which is Presbyterian but not the Presbyterian Church in Canada. Users will filter on "Presbyterian" and expect both. Store the specific body, map it to a filterable family.

Geocode with OSM even though the map is Google

All nine addresses geocoded cleanly against Nominatim — eight on the first try, and every one resolved to an OSM place_of_worship building rather than an interpolated street address, so the pins land on the church and not the middle of the road. Postal codes came back as a by-product. The reason to prefer this over Google's geocoder isn't the API key: Google Maps Platform terms restrict persisting geocoded coordinates in your own database, which is exactly what a seed dataset does. OSM data is ODbL — store it indefinitely, credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors". Rendering OSM-derived coordinates on a Google map is fine, so this costs nothing.

Crawling the church's own site beats searching for it

Service times and sermon feeds look nearly absent in this table, but that's an artifact of method — I searched rather than crawled. The one church whose homepage I actually fetched gave up both service times and two sermon feeds in a single request. Point the collector at website_url from Places and the last two columns fill in.

Still open: three of these ten sit within one block of Bond Street — St. Michael's, St. George, and First Lutheran — which will collide as map pins at city zoom. Worth deciding on clustering before the map work in step 1.

Also unresolved: no church in the sample lands in the 2000+ age bucket. Downtown Toronto skews historic, so a curated seed of the core will leave that filter looking broken. Suburban plants would populate it — worth checking before committing to downtown-first coverage.
